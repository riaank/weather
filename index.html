    How the consensus is worked out

    Temperature and wind speed. A weighted mean of every source with a value for that hour. When five or more report, the single highest and lowest values are dropped first, so one outlier model can't drag the result.

    Wind direction. Averaged as vectors rather than degrees, so 350° and 10° average to north, not south. When models point different ways, the day's agreement drops.

    Chance of rain. Four equal votes: the weighted share of single models showing rain that hour, and the share of runs showing rain in each of the ECMWF, GFS and ICON ensembles. Rain amounts are the median of the sources that have rain, because averaging wet and dry models smears a short shower into all-day drizzle.

    Timing. Each hour is labelled by when it starts, so 14:00 means rain between 14:00 and 15:00. On narrow screens the 7-day view groups hours into 3-hour blocks and shows the highest chance in each block. Sources that only give rain in 3- or 6-hour blocks are left out of rain timing but still count for temperature and wind.

    Agreement. Combines how closely models match on temperature, how decisive the rain signal is, and whether wind directions line up.

    Weights. Start equal, except Yr.no and AccuWeather at 0.5, because both lean heavily on ECMWF and would otherwise count it twice. These are judgement calls, not measured skill: nothing here checks forecasts against what actually happened yet.
